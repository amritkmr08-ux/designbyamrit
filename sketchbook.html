Sketchbook.

The small screens behind the big case studies.

Screens from contract software with AI in it, designed between 2023 and 2026. The case studies tell the story. This page shows the parts: a card, a panel, a message, a confirm step. Eight groups, one idea each.

Design files, not screenshots of the live product. Company and people names are made up. Click any image to see it bigger.

01The answer comes with its source

Ask in plain words. Get the answer, the exact text it came from, and a way to say no.

When AI pulls a value out of a contract, the page and passage sit right next to it, so you can check before you trust. A clause the AI wrote has accept, discard and refine under it. You never leave the screen to decide.

02Why, not just what

When AI says two company names are the same company, it shows its reasoning.

Which words matched, which ones it ignored (Ltd, Inc, Pvt), and the evidence it used. Progress is shown as named steps, not a spinner. When nothing matches, the screen offers two next moves instead of an error.

03A person gets the last word

AI groups the different spellings of a company. A person checks each group and confirms once.

Every group shows how many documents it holds. Any group can be undone on the spot. A missing company can be added without leaving the flow, and the empty state says what will show up here and why.

04How contracts relate, on the row itself

Applies to, changes, replaces. Read it on the record instead of guessing from a folder.

A contract card says what it governs, what it sits under, how long it runs, and what an amendment changed. A clause review counts how much has been checked. One screen explains itself without a second one.

05A date that explains itself

The notice deadline is worked out from the end date. When the end date moved, the deadline moved with it, and the screen says so.

The card shows the rule, the clauses behind it, and a history of every change, including the ones the system made on its own. Confidence is shown as high, medium or low rather than a percentage, and a filter counts the items that still need a human look.

06Check the AI before switching it on

A new AI version arrives as pending. It goes live only after a test.

Three steps: pick data that looks like real work, pick what to score, run it. Each saved version keeps its result. When a project is created, the setup asks in plain words whether the agent should run and what it will do.

07The moments in between

Thinking, working, done. The states where most products go quiet.

A thinking line that says what it is thinking about. Working notices in two sizes that say what comes next. A done message that names what got done. Small text, but it is where trust is kept or lost.

08The boring parts, done carefully

Menus, filters, bulk actions, sharing.

A column picker you can search. A view menu with delete kept away from the rest. A confirm step before anything is destroyed. A bulk bar that counts what is selected. A share dialog with internal and outside people in one list. Nothing clever, which is the point.
