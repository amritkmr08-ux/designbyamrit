Design thinking · Sirion as an agentic platform

Does relational intelligence
need to be seen,
or only known?

The central design question for Sirion's next evolution. If AI can answer every contract question through natural language — which amendment, which MSA, which SOW — does the relationship graph need a visual surface at all? Or is it enough that the structure exists beneath, powering reliable answers?

Position A
The graph is backend infrastructure. Users ask, AI answers. No need to expose the structure visually.
vs
Position B
Users need to see, verify, edit, and trust the structure. Invisible intelligence is unaccountable intelligence.
01 · The argument

The answer is both —
but why matters enormously

The question "should we expose the relationship graph or keep it backend?" is actually the wrong frame. The right frame is: what does the user need to do that requires seeing structure? And conversely: what is better handled by intelligence that works silently?

Here is the honest answer: in a fully mature agentic system — one where AI is reliable, fast, and trusted — many structural questions disappear as UI problems. You don't need to see a graph of which amendment applies to this SOW if you can just ask and get a correct, cited, confident answer in 2 seconds.

But we are not there yet. And even when we get there, three categories of users will always need the visual surface: those who need to verify, those who need to edit, and those who need to govern. This is not a transitional design decision — it is a permanent one.

"When a legal team member is about to sign off on an obligation that depends on knowing which amendment applies — do they trust an AI answer, or do they need to see the relationship themselves?"
→ They need to see it. Accountability requires visibility. AI answers require evidence.

The insight that resolves this debate: AI answers need to be backed by visible structure, not instead of visible structure. When AskSirion says "Amendment 4 applies to your Boston SOW," the user's next action is to click through and verify that relationship in the structure view. The UI is the citation. The structure view is the footnote to every AI answer.

02 · Exposure spectrum

The spectrum of
how much to surface

There is no binary choice between "show the graph" and "hide it." There is a spectrum of exposure. Where Sirion sits on that spectrum — and where it should move toward — is a product design decision with real consequences.

Pure Backend
Fully Visual
1
Invisible Graph
Structure exists in database only. AI queries it. User never sees it. ChatGPT model. Works when AI is 100% trusted.
2
Answer with Citation
AI answers, adds source reference: "Based on MSA CO234 → Amendment 4." User can click through but often won't.
3
Structured List View
Current Sirion redesign direction. Accordion sections, relationship chips, entity metadata panel. Structure visible, scannable.
4
Relationship Panel
Right panel shows typed relationships for any selected entity. "Governed by, modifies, applies to." Relationships explicit but not graph-visual.
5
Graph Canvas
Interactive node-edge visualization of the contract family. Users see the actual graph. Powerful for power users, complex to implement well.

The current redesign (Level 3) is the right starting point. Level 4 — relationship panel per entity — should be the next layer. Level 5 (graph canvas) is powerful but should be an optional "expert mode" or "contract family map" view, not the primary interface. Most users will never need it. But some will need it desperately.

03 · Future scenarios

Imagining Sirion as a
fully agentic platform —
what changes?

Imagine it is 2027. Sirion's AI is highly reliable. An agent can read, interpret, and reason about any contract in your portfolio. It can autonomously detect that Amendment 4 has been superseded, that a SOW is about to expire, that a counterparty's obligation is being breached. What does the product look like? And where does the visual relationship structure fit?

S — 01 The Autonomous Risk Alert Both layers
The agentic moment
Sirion agent monitors portfolio. Detects: Amendment 5 was executed last week. It supersedes Amendment 3. Three SOWs were still referencing Amendment 3 terms in open obligation tracking. Agent fires alert: "Amendment 3 is superseded. 3 obligations may be calculated incorrectly."
What the user needs to do
User receives alert. Wants to verify before acting. Clicks through to contract structure. Sees: Amendment 5 node, "Supersedes: Amendment 3" relationship, "Applies to: SOW Boston, SOW Tokyo, SOW Chicago" relationships highlighted. Then confirms the fix.
The agent does the detection. The structure view does the verification. Both are required. Neither replaces the other. The relationship graph is the audit trail for agentic decisions.
S — 02 The "Ask Anything" Contract Query Backend only — mostly
The agentic moment
User types: "Which amendments have been applied to the APAC SOWs in the last 6 months, and what did each one change?" Agent traverses relationship graph, pulls amendment nodes with "applies to" APAC SOWs, reads each amendment's scope metadata, returns a structured answer with citations.
Does UI structure help here?
Mostly no — the AI answer is faster and better formatted than the user navigating structure themselves. But the answer includes deep links to each amendment node. User who wants to verify opens the structure view for that specific amendment. The graph is available, not forced.
For query-and-retrieve tasks: backend intelligence is primary. Visual structure is the verification layer. Design implication: AI answers should always cite the relationship they traversed, with a one-click path to view it.
S — 03 Relationship Conflict Detection UI surface required
The agentic moment
Agent detects: Two amendments both claim to "modify pricing Section 4.2" of the MSA. Amendment 6 and Amendment 7 have overlapping scope. Neither supersedes the other. There is a genuine legal ambiguity. Agent cannot resolve this — it flags it.
What the user must do
This is a legal decision. A human must review both amendments, understand the overlap, decide which governs, and update the relationship model to reflect the resolution. The user must see the relationship structure here. AI can surface the conflict, but cannot resolve it.
AI detects. Human decides. Structure is the workspace for the decision. The relationship UI is the place where legal humans exercise their judgment. It cannot be a backend-only concern.
S — 04 New Contract Ingestion — Relationship Assignment Both layers
The agentic moment
A new Amendment PDF is uploaded. Sirion agent reads it, extracts: parties match MSA CO234, references "Section 3.4 of the Master Service Agreement," effective date is after Amendment 5. Agent suggests: "This appears to modify CO234 MSA, superseding Amendment 5. Confidence: 91%."
What the user needs to do
User sees suggested relationship in structure view with a "System suggested — confirm?" state. Reviews the relationship. Clicks confirm. Or reassigns. Or marks as "relates to" instead of "modifies." The structure view is the confirmation interface for AI relationship suggestions.
This is the most important design scenario. AI suggests relationships. Humans confirm or correct. The visual structure is the interface for relationship governance — human oversight of machine inference. This is non-negotiable.
S — 05 Portfolio-Level Relationship Map UI surface required
The scenario
A General Counsel needs to understand: across all 12 suppliers in the APAC region, which MSAs are expiring in Q2, how many SOWs depend on each, and whether any of those SOWs have amendments in draft state? This is a portfolio governance question, not a single-contract question.
What the design must provide
A graph or structured visual that surfaces the full relationship picture across the portfolio. Not a table of contracts. Not a folder list. A visual that shows the dependency web — which entities are anchored to which, what is at risk if an MSA expires, what is pending.
AI cannot replace a strategic overview visualization. A GC does not want to ask 20 queries. They want to see the portfolio relationship map and make a decision. The graph canvas (Level 5) exists precisely for this user. Design it as the "command center" view.
S — 06 Agentic Negotiation Support Near-future agentic
The 2027 scenario
A procurement team is renegotiating with a supplier. An agent briefs them before the call: "Current MSA CO234 governs 540 SOWs. 3 amendments are active. Amendment 5 introduced a pricing cap that expires in 90 days. The supplier's proposed new amendment conflicts with Amendment 3's exclusivity clause." This briefing is entirely AI-generated from relationship data.
The design requirement
The briefing document itself must be relationship-structured, not just prose. Each claim must be clickable to its source relationship node. During negotiation, the user should be able to pull up the relationship structure to verify a specific clause's authority chain. The structure view serves as a negotiation reference.
The relationship structure becomes a live reference during high-stakes moments. Not just for historical analysis — for real-time decision support. Design implication: structure must be accessible in 1 click from anywhere, including AI-generated briefings.
04 · Design prescription

What to build —
specifically

The relational graph must be exposed — but at the right fidelity, in the right moments, for the right users.
Layer 1 (Build now): Structured list view with relationship chips and authority-first accordion. This is the current redesign. Ship it. It solves the daily user problem immediately.
Layer 2 (Build next): Relationship confirmation UI — the interface for humans to verify, correct, and govern AI-suggested relationships. This is not a nice-to-have. It is the accountability layer.
Layer 3 (Build after): AI answer citations with deep-link to structure. Every AskSirion answer should show which relationship it traversed, with one click to see it in context. Trust requires traceability.
Layer 4 (Build for power users): Graph canvas / contract family map. An expert-mode visual for GCs and legal ops leads who need to see the full portfolio dependency graph. Not the primary UI — an optional view mode.

05 · Design principles

The principles that govern
how to expose this

Principle 01
Intelligence must be accountable
Every AI answer about contract relationships must be traceable to the relationship data that produced it. Never let AI make a structural claim without a visible citation. The structure view is the accountability layer — it is not optional, it is the audit trail.
When AskSirion says "Amendment 4 applies to this SOW" → show: "Based on: CO9142 SOW → Applied Amendments → Amendment 4 (Effective Feb 2024)" with a click to verify.
Principle 02
AI suggests. Humans govern.
The system infers relationships from document content, metadata, party matching, and date sequences. But the legal authority for what a relationship means — whether Amendment 5 truly supersedes Amendment 3 — belongs to humans. Design for this transfer of authority explicitly. Never make it implicit.
New contract uploaded → Agent suggests relationship → User sees "System suggested (91% confidence)" → One-action confirm or reassign. The confirmation is recorded in audit log.
Principle 03
Structure answers "what is true." AI answers "tell me about it."
These are complementary surfaces, not competing ones. The structure view is for when a user needs to verify, navigate, or govern. The AI assistant is for when a user needs to query, summarize, or be briefed. Design them to reinforce each other, not replace each other.
Structure view answers: "Show me all amendments to this MSA." AI assistant answers: "Summarize what changed across all amendments to this MSA." Both use the same graph. Different interface for different intent.
Principle 04
Complexity on demand, not by default
A contract manager reviewing a single SOW does not need to see the full graph canvas. They need to see that SOW's governing MSA and applied amendments. A GC doing portfolio governance needs the full picture. Design for progressive disclosure — simple first, complex on demand. Never front-load the graph.
Default view: structured list with relationship chips. Click "View family map" → graph canvas opens in a panel or overlay. Power users find it. Casual users never need it.
Principle 05
The graph is the product, not a feature
Every piece of Sirion — obligations tracking, risk scoring, negotiation support, compliance monitoring — should be able to query the relationship graph. The graph is not a UI component on one screen. It is the central data model that powers everything. Design decisions about how to expose it should treat it with that gravity.
Obligation tracking knows "this obligation is in SOW Boston, which is governed by MSA CO234, which has Amendment 5 active, which modifies payment terms." Without the graph, obligation tracking is a list of clauses. With it, it is contextual intelligence.
Principle 06
Agentic actions must be stoppable
As Sirion becomes more agentic — automatically detecting supersessions, filing alerts, suggesting changes — the UI must give humans a clear way to review and reverse any agentic action that touched the relationship model. The graph is a legal record. Agents can read it freely. Writing to it requires human confirmation.
"Agent proposed: Mark Amendment 3 as superseded by Amendment 6. Review and confirm?" Not: "Amendment 3 has been marked superseded." Always ask before writing to legal structure.
06 · Time horizon

What the platform looks like
across three futures

The role of the visual relationship surface changes as the platform evolves. Understanding these three states helps prioritize what to build now versus what to design toward.

Now → 2026 · Sirion Today
The Meaning Layer
The primary design job is replacing folder metaphors with relationship-first structure. Users actively navigate the contract family. The UI carries most of the intelligence. AI is a query assistant that benefits from but does not drive the structure.

The relationship graph is the primary UI. Most user value comes from being able to see it and scan it correctly.
Designer's job now Build the authority-first accordion. Relationship chips. Entity metadata panel. Relationship confirmation UI. Make the structure visible and scannable. This is the foundation everything else stands on.
2026 → 2028 · Transitional Agentic
The Verification Layer
AI is answering many questions correctly and fast. Users are increasingly trusting AI for first-pass queries. But the structure view becomes the verification and governance layer — where users go to confirm, correct, and audit what AI has inferred or acted on.

The graph is still exposed but it is increasingly the exception not the default interaction. AskSirion is often the entry point; structure view is the depth dive.
Designer's job then AI-to-structure citation design. Relationship governance workflows. Conflict resolution UI. Agentic action confirmation patterns. The human oversight layer for an increasingly autonomous system.
2028+ · Mature Agentic Platform
The Command Layer
The platform is genuinely agentic. Agents monitor, detect, suggest, and execute. Most daily questions are answered by AI with high confidence and full citations. The visual structure surface is used primarily by power users, governance roles, and for high-stakes decisions.

The graph becomes a command center for portfolio governance — not navigation for individual contracts.
Designer's job then Portfolio intelligence visualization. Cross-portfolio relationship maps. Agentic workflow monitoring UIs. Strategic overview for GCs and CLOs. The graph canvas as a command center, not a navigation tool.
07 · Agentic design patterns

Specific design patterns
for the agentic era

These are the new UI patterns that do not exist in today's enterprise software but will be table stakes for an agentic contract platform. These are the things to design toward.

🔍
Relationship Citation in AI Answers
"Which amendment applies to SOW Boston?"
→ "Amendment 4 (CO9142). [Based on: SOW Boston → Applied Amendments → Amendment 4, confirmed by user Priya on 14 Jan 2025]"
Design pattern
Every AI answer shows the graph path it traversed. Clicking the citation opens the specific relationship in structure view, highlighted. The citation is not just a source link — it is a graph path visualization.
⚡
Relationship Suggestion Card
New document uploaded → agent reads → suggests: "This appears to be an amendment to CO234 MSA, superseding Amendment 5. Confidence: 91%. Reason: Party match + date sequence + reference to Section 4.2."
Design pattern
A specialized card in the structure view shows pending relationship suggestions from the agent. Each has confidence score, reasoning, and three actions: Confirm / Reassign / Reject. Confirmed suggestions update the graph. This card is the human-AI handoff zone.
⚠️
Relationship Conflict Flag
Agent detects: Amendment 6 and Amendment 7 both claim to modify pricing Section 4.2. Neither supersedes the other. Legal ambiguity detected. Human decision required.
Design pattern
A conflict state appears on both amendment nodes in the structure view. A conflict resolution panel opens showing both amendments side by side, the overlapping scope, and options: "Set Amendment 7 as primary / Mark as coexisting / Escalate for legal review." Agent cannot close this — only a human can.
🗺️
Contract Family Map
GC opens portfolio view for APAC supplier relationships before board meeting. Needs to see: which MSAs expire in Q2, how many SOWs depend on each, amendment activity in last 90 days.
Design pattern
A graph canvas view showing the contract family as a node-edge map. Nodes are sized by TCV. Edges are colored by relationship type. Expiry status shown as visual urgency. One click from any node opens the structured list view for that entity. This is the GC's morning dashboard.
📋
Agentic Action Log
Agent flagged Amendment 3 as superseded, updated 3 SOW obligation calculations, and sent alerts to 2 contract managers — all overnight. User needs to understand and review what changed.
Design pattern
A relationship timeline view shows every change to the graph — who/what changed it, when, and why. Human-confirmed changes and agent-suggested changes are distinguished. Any agentic change can be rolled back. The timeline is the auditability surface for an agentic system.
📖
Pre-Negotiation Briefing
Procurement team has a call with supplier in 2 hours. Agent generates: current governing structure, active amendments, expiring terms, known disputes, counterparty's history across all contracts.
Design pattern
A briefing document generated from the relationship graph. Each section cites the node it came from. Users can click any claim to see the underlying contract node. During the call, the structure view is accessible as a sidebar reference. The briefing is dynamic — linked to live data, not a static export.
"The question is not whether to expose the relationship graph. The question is: at which moments does a human need to see it, verify it, govern it, or override it? Design for those moments — and make everything else invisible."
— The design direction for Sirion's relational intelligence layer
