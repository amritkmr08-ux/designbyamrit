How I think

02

01

Surface ambiguity, don't hide it

A system that hides its uncertainty spends trust it hasn't earned. Low confidence should look different from high confidence — always.

02

Keep reasoning one click away

Trust isn't asked for, it's inspectable. Every value should trace back to the source it came from, without leaving the flow.

03

Reserve judgment for where it changes the outcome

Asking a human to confirm everything is the same as asking them to do everything. Ask by exception; act everywhere else.

About

03

Most AI features fail at the same question: when should the system explain itself, act on its own, or ask permission? That's the question I design around — systems that show their reasoning, reserve human judgment for where it changes the outcome, and know when to disappear.

I work in contract and legal software, where errors have real consequences and users are professional skeptics. And I prototype in working code, so ideas prove themselves in the browser — not just in Figma.

My mission at Sirion has been simple: make complexity feel calm. Legal teams carry the weight of precision, and they don't have time to figure out an interface.

I came to product design the long way round — years in brand, creative direction and co-founding a denim label before moving into digital products. That history shows up in the work: I care as much about how something reads as how it functions.

Design is personal for me. Being a father changed how I see it — I watch how naturally my daughter explores, where she gets confused, how she delights in small wins. That's the same lens I bring to work. When design steps back and lets someone move forward with confidence, that's when it's working.
