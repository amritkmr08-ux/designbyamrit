What I keep coming back to

Being sure and being allowed are two different things.

An AI can be confident and still not have permission to act. It predicts the answer; a person owns the consequences. I design for that gap.

An agent should show what it understood before you trust it. The listing agent restates your request as removable chips. You correct it directly. Where this shipped → Personalisation reorders the page. It never edits the truth. Two roles see the same facts, differently ranked. A hidden clause reads as a clause that doesn't exist. The argument → Ask permission at the irreversible step. Only there. Drafting can be undone; sending cannot. One real decision instead of supervising twelve small ones. The rule →
About

At Sirion, I try to make complexity feel calm. Legal teams already have to get difficult decisions right. The interface shouldn't make that harder.

I came to product design through brand, creative direction, and a denim label I co-founded. That background shows up in the work: I care how something reads as much as how it works, and I'd rather prove an idea in a browser than in a deck.

Being a father changed how I see design. I watch where my daughter gets stuck and what she tries next — I look for the same things at work. Good design gets out of the way so people can keep going.
