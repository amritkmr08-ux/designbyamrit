Contract Relationships

Contract systems · Sirion · 2025–26

A folder tells you where a document sits. Not what it changes.

A legal team asks “which amendment applies to this SOW?” dozens of times a week. Answering took half an hour of reconstruction: open the folder, read the file names, check the notes, and hope nobody had renamed anything.

It was never a search problem

I tried what search offers: filters, OCR, a better index. None of it worked.

Users weren’t searching. They were reconstructing meaning. A search result hands you a document. What they needed was a sentence: this amendment, from this date, governs this SOW, and supersedes the one before it.

The argument I had to win

The agreed direction was to improve the folder hierarchy: faster, safer, shippable in weeks, and defensible because the whole category works that way. It was already in the product, and customers were asking for it by name.

I argued the hierarchy was the problem. An amendment can govern several agreements at once; a record can inherit from more than one instrument. Contract relationships are many to many. A tree cannot express them.

This was not a preference between two layouts. A folder tree can only ever answer where a file is stored, so every feature built above it inherits that limit.

Why a design argument was an AI argument

A wrong model doesn’t just inconvenience a user. It makes the AI answer with the superseded clause in the same confident tone it uses for the right one, because nothing in the structure tells it which one governs.

What a system can represent sets the ceiling on what any model built over it can understand. That is now the argument I make before anyone opens a design tool.

What I designed

Navigation and meaning became two different things. A record carries explicit relationships — governed by, amended by, applies to — and the interface reads them back in words: which agreement governs this one, which amendment currently applies, what it changed, and what it superseded.

Open a statement of work and the panel answers the question the team was asking, rather than showing you the shelf the file was on.

And it had to survive real volume

A relationship view is easy to draw for a family of six. Real families aren’t six. I built it as a working prototype and ran it at 340 statements of work.

The radial layout shrank to 12% to fit and left zero legible labels. I replaced it with branching columns grouped by status rather than defend it, and settled the debate with the measurement instead of an opinion.

What transfers

Any system where AI reasons over stored records. If the storage model is wrong, the AI is confidently wrong. Fix the model before the interface.

My role: framing the problem, prototyping the alternatives, making the argument · Shared decisions with designers, product managers and engineers · Approved and prioritised for build; not in production, so there are no post-launch numbers here

Contract Onboarding

AI onboarding review · Sirion · Shipped

The agent groups the contracts. A person decides the groups are right.

A new customer arrives with years of contracts as loose files. Before the platform can track a single renewal or obligation, every file needs two facts attached: who it is with, and which agreement it belongs under. Someone used to read each one to find out.

This is the first thing a customer ever does with the product. If it goes badly, everything built on top of it inherits the mess.

The speed is the value and the risk

The agent reads thousands of documents in the time a person reads one. At that speed a wrong assumption is applied thousands of times before anyone sees it.

So the checkpoints go inside the run, not after it: review what was extracted, review the counterparty mapping, review the contract families. The agent proposes at each one; a person confirms or corrects, and the run continues from there rather than starting over.

Review by counterparty, not by document

Three documents the agent believes are with the same company are one row that opens to its members. A wrong name gets fixed once, not three times.

It sounds like a grouping decision. It is a decision about whose time the interface spends: the reviewer’s job becomes confirming a judgement, not repeating a correction.

The screen splits the work into what is mapped, what is missing a match, and what is orphaned, so the reviewer always knows how much is left and what kind of decision each pile needs.

It explains itself in words, not a score

Open the reasoning for a match and it says why two names were treated as one company, and quotes the passage it read. No percentage.

A person can check a sentence. They cannot check 0.87.

Bulk, without losing the thread

Confirming a group applies to every document in it, and the system offers the system records it thinks are right rather than making the reviewer search. Where nothing fits, they create the record from the same place instead of leaving the flow.

What transfers

Any bulk AI process a person is accountable for. Put the human checkpoints inside the run, group the work by the unit the person actually decides about, and make one correction fix everything downstream of it.

Phoenix Listing

Agent in the contract listing · Sirion · In development

A question reshapes the contract listing into the answer.

Enterprise software is designed as though people will arrive at it. They don’t.

Nobody’s job is “check the contract system”. A system of record isn’t somewhere anyone chooses to spend their morning, and designing as if they will is a large part of why this category still feels manual.

The first question is not what the homepage shows

If we can’t rely on people coming to us, how does the work reach them? That reframes the surface: the listing is not a place to browse, it is where a question gets answered and a decision gets made.

Four situations, not four features

Not personas. States of mind. The same person moves between them hourly, and which one they are in decides whether the agent should explain, suggest, ask or act.

They already know what they want and just need it done. They don’t know yet and need the system to bring the problem to them. They are looking for something specific. They are checking whether anything is wrong. Each one calls for a different amount of agent autonomy, and getting that wrong is what makes AI features feel either useless or alarming.

I wrote that as a rule set, argued it to executive stakeholders, and it was adopted as direction for the platform. This listing is the first surface built on it.

What it does with a question

You ask in the panel beside the list. The agent shows the steps it took — reading the intent, setting the criteria, finding the contracts — then reads back what it understood and answers in the same place.

Then it stops and asks what to do next: tag these for the executive dashboard, or send the summary to the accounts team. It has the answer and it still waits. Being sure and being allowed are two different things.

Why the readback matters

A wrong answer that arrives with its reasoning visible is recoverable. A wrong answer that arrives as a finished action is not. The panel is designed so the expensive step always happens after a person has seen the interpretation.

What I own here

The framework and the argument for it, the sessions with stakeholders, and the listing prototypes in HTML. Scope is platform-wide: entry points, personalisation, how much autonomy an agent gets, and how we would know it worked.

Auto Extraction

AI review interface · Sirion · Shipped 2024

An AI can extract 128 fields. A lawyer still has to check every one.

The model pulled 128 values out of a contract in seconds. A lawyer then had to check every one.

If checking takes as long as reading, the AI has moved the work, not removed it. That was the whole design problem, and it is not solved by making the model better.

Three versions, each one earning the next

Highlights in the text: the values were findable, but review became a hunt through the document. Hover cards: closer, but the answer vanished the moment you looked away, so nothing accumulated.

Assist bars in the margin, permanently visible: 128 scattered values become one pass down the document. Each version fixed the last one’s complaint and exposed a new one. That is the honest shape of the work.

The decision I’d defend hardest

We removed the confidence score. A lawyer cannot act on 0.82.

The card shows the model’s reasoning and the passage the value came from instead, so verification becomes reading — which is what lawyers are trained to do, and what they are paid for.

Accept, and the document comes to you

Accepting a value marks it and opens the next one, and the document scrolls to the passage that field was read from. The reviewer never goes looking; the work arrives in order and the position in the document is always earned by the field in hand.

Edit and reject sit beside accept, at the same weight, because disagreeing has to be as cheap as agreeing or people stop reading and start clicking.

What happened

Month-four retention went from 5% to 23%. The redesign didn’t win more users. It stopped losing the ones who arrived, which is the outcome that matters for a tool people are assigned rather than choose.

Two eight-month cohorts in Heap, captured June 2025. The cohort mix and the extraction model both changed over the period, so I claim the shape of the curve, not the whole number.

What transfers

Wherever a model produces many answers a person is accountable for: show one at a time, put the evidence next to the claim, and spend the interface on verification rather than on presenting confidence.
